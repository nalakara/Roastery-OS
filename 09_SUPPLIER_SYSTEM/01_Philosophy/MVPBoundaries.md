# MVP Boundaries

## Purpose

This document defines the MVP boundaries and implementation scope limitations for the Supplier System inside Roastery OS.

The purpose of MVP Boundaries is to:
- preserve architectural focus,
- maintain operational realism,
- avoid premature enterprise procurement complexity,
- and establish a scalable sourcing continuity foundation.

The MVP Supplier System should prioritize:
- deterministic supplier continuity,
- sourcing relationship visibility,
- procurement explainability,
- operational resilience awareness,
- and human-readable supplier continuity.

The MVP is intended to establish:
- a strong upstream sourcing foundation,
not:
- enterprise procurement governance infrastructure.

---

# Core Philosophy

Roastery OS evolves progressively.

The MVP Supplier System should therefore focus on:
- practical sourcing continuity,
- explainable procurement relationships,
- operational supplier visibility,
- and deterministic sourcing genealogy.

The MVP should avoid:
- enterprise procurement bureaucracy,
- industrial supply chain orchestration,
- and premature AI autonomy.

The goal is:
- operational usefulness first,
- ecosystem sophistication later.

Human-Centered MVP Principle
The MVP should remain:

	•	understandable,
	•	and operationally useful.
Operators should be able to:
	•	understand sourcing continuity,
	•	evaluate supplier relationships,
	•	and trace procurement ancestry without enterprise ERP complexity.
The MVP should avoid:
	•	procurement bureaucracy,
	•	compliance intimidation,
	•	and excessive operational abstraction.

Simplicity Principle
The MVP should prioritize:
	•	sourcing clarity,
	•	procurement visibility,
	•	and deterministic operational relationships.
Complexity should only be introduced when:
	•	operational value clearly justifies it.
Architecture should remain:
	•	scalable internally, while staying:
	•	simple externally.

Modularity Principle
The MVP should preserve:
	•	modular sourcing architecture,
	•	deterministic procurement foundations,
	•	and future extensibility.
Even if advanced systems are excluded initially, the architecture should already support future evolution toward:
	•	AI-assisted procurement intelligence,
	•	predictive sourcing analytics,
	•	supplier reliability forecasting,
	•	supply chain transparency,
	•	and ecosystem-wide sourcing continuity.
The MVP should establish:
	•	a stable sourcing continuity foundation, not:
	•	temporary procurement logic.
